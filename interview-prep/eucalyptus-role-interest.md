# What interests you most about this role / working for Eucalyptus?

---

Honestly, three things stood out to me — the mission, the engineering challenges, and where the company is headed.

First, the mission. I've spent the last few years building products at scale — an enterprise analytics platform serving 10+ organisations, a loan matching system, an AI microservice that cut manual work by 90%. The work was technically challenging, and I'm proud of it, but at the end of the day, I was helping brokers write notes faster. Eucalyptus is helping over half a million patients access healthcare they couldn't easily get before — whether that's through Pilot, Juniper, Kin, or Software. That's a fundamentally different kind of impact, and it's the kind of problem I want my engineering work to solve.

Second, the technical problems here are genuinely interesting to me — and they line up closely with what I've been doing. At youXpowered, I architected a multi-tenant analytics platform that had to work differently for each organisation while running on a shared codebase. Eucalyptus is doing something similar with multiple brands on one configurable platform, and that kind of architecture is something I've already navigated and find deeply engaging. I also built real-time dashboards with 60-second refresh intervals and cross-filtering across 25+ report types — so the idea of working with federated GraphQL, microservices, and Pub/Sub in a high-velocity deployment environment where code hits production within minutes of merge — that's exactly where I want to be. And the stack — TypeScript, React, Node.js — that's my bread and butter. I've worked with AWS; picking up GCP is a natural transition, not a leap.

Third, the trajectory. Eucalyptus is doing over $120 million in revenue, expanding across 9 countries, and the Hims & Hers acquisition validates the business at a billion-dollar level. What excites me about that isn't just the scale — it's the type of engineering problems that come with it. Multi-region infrastructure, international expansion, regulatory compliance across different markets — these are the kinds of challenges that push you to become a significantly better engineer. And at this stage of the company, individual engineers still have outsized impact, which matters a lot to me. At youXpowered, I solely owned the complete lifecycle of youXapply and led a team of 8 on youXinsights. I want to bring that same ownership and initiative here.

Ultimately, I want to be somewhere the engineering is hard, the mission matters, and what I build actually reaches people. Eucalyptus is that place.
